# The Value of Open Research Information: Nine Transformative Benefits

**Barcelona Declaration Working Group 7 - Task Force 2** **Version:** 0.2 (Brief) **Date:** February 2026, revised August 2026

**Open Research Information (ORI)** offers a solution: structured, machine-readable metadata about research actors, activities, inputs, outputs, and impacts, made openly available through open infrastructures using standard protocols and identifiers.

The Barcelona Declaration commits signatories to: (1) **making openness the default**, (2) **working with open systems**, (3) **supporting infrastructure sustainability**, and (4) **enabling collective action**.

**These commitments unlock:**

## **The Nine Benefits: Three Transformative Axes**

### **AXIS I: QUALITY & TRUST**

#### **B1: Research Output Quality & Traceability**

Traditional metrics count publications but do not capture the research process. ORI supports reproducible research by making data, methods, versions, and relationships between research objects explicit across the research lifecycle, enabling verification and trust.  
**Who benefits:** Researchers can verify and build on each other’s work; institutions demonstrate quality beyond citations; funders track outputs systematically.

#### **B2: Governance, Ethics & Compliance**

Ethical practices and compliance are invisible in publication counts. ORI enables transparent research conduct monitoring, ethics approval tracking, data protection verification, and institutional accountability for how research is conducted, following "as open as possible, as closed as necessary."  
**Who benefits:** Research institutions demonstrate responsible practices; funders verify ethical alignment; policymakers ensure regulatory compliance.

#### **B8: Integrity & Accountability**

Traditional metrics cannot reveal ethical issues or conflicts of interest. ORI provides research misconduct detection and prevention, transparent funding disclosure, comprehensive audit trails, and public accountability mechanisms.  
**Who benefits:** Research communities build trust through visible practices; evaluators detect integrity issues early; civil society monitors research accountability.

### **AXIS II: COLLABORATION & INNOVATION**

#### **B3: Capacity Building & Inclusive Training**

Learning processes and skill development are absent from traditional metrics. ORI makes visible skills development in open research practices, metadata literacy programs, support for diverse researchers, FAIR data training, and institutional capacity strengthening.  
**Who benefits:** Early-career researchers gain recognition for growing expertise; underrepresented communities receive targeted support; institutions build sustainable open science capabilities.

#### **B4: Interoperability & Metadata Quality**

Infrastructure enabling research connectivity is invisible in traditional metrics. ORI promotes harmonized metadata schemas, persistent identifier (PID) adoption, standardized vocabularies, system integration and APIs, and cross-platform data exchange.  
**Who benefits:** Infrastructure providers ensure system interoperability; researchers access data across institutional boundaries; libraries and data stewards maintain high-quality metadata.

#### **B7: Infrastructure & Technical Enablement**

Technical foundations enabling research are invisible in traditional output metrics. ORI supports sustainable infrastructure development, technical standards implementation, service interoperability, platform reliability, and innovation in research tools.  
**Who benefits:** Technology providers develop sustainable platforms; research infrastructure organizations gain recognition for essential work; institutions justify infrastructure investments.

#### **B9: Strategic Steering & Institutional Autonomy**

Traditional metrics say nothing about what research costs or about who supplies the information used to decide. ORI makes the scholarly communication system itself observable: the costs and terms of publishing, the conduct and performance of venues, and dependency on proprietary data sources. That lets institutions steer rather than merely comply, reallocating resources towards community-led and Diamond infrastructure, reducing supplier lock-in, and supporting informed publishing choices.  
**Who benefits:** Institutions decide where to invest on their own evidence rather than a supplier's; libraries and research managers can justify a budget shift; community-led and Diamond infrastructures become fundable on visible grounds; researchers choose venues on transparent terms; funders and policy makers see the real economics of scholarly communication.

### **AXIS III: IMPACT & RELEVANCE**

#### **B5: Evaluation for Learning & Responsibility**

Traditional metrics drive competitive ranking rather than learning. ORI enables formative assessment approaches, multi-dimensional impact evaluation, equity-focused evaluation frameworks, organizational learning mechanisms, and responsible research assessment practices.  
**Who benefits:** Evaluators assess research quality beyond output counts; institutions learn what works and why; researchers receive developmental feedback rather than just judgment.

#### **B6: Visibility, Equity & Societal Relevance**

Societal impact and public engagement are undervalued in traditional metrics. ORI enhances research accessibility for non-academic audiences, citizen science enablement, geographic and linguistic equity, SDG alignment tracking, and public engagement.  
**Who benefits:** Civil society organizations access relevant research; citizen scientists contribute meaningfully; policymakers connect research to societal priorities; communities worldwide participate in research.

## **Changes in 0.2 (August 2026)**

- **Added B9: Strategic Steering & Institutional Autonomy** (Axis II, Sustainable
  Innovation). The brief did not use the word "strategy" once, and its only economic term
  was "institutions justify infrastructure investments". It described what open information
  makes visible about research, and had no place for information about the system that
  publishes research, nor for an institution acting on it.
- **Narrowed B2** by removing "open science policy implementation". That clause absorbed
  almost any institutional case, because almost every institutional case is a policy being
  implemented. The two changes only make sense together.

Both came out of placing a real case against the framework: the University of Milan
response in the WG7 case study collection, on nine years of APC monitoring and the funding
decisions it drove. Five things in that case had no benefit to land on. They are one gap
seen from five sides, and B9 is that gap.
