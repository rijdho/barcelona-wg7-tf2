# Taxonomy of Open Research Information: Stakeholders and Benefits
## Barcelona Declaration Working Group 7 - Task Force 2

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** January 2026
**Status:** Working Document

---

## Executive Summary

This taxonomy provides a comprehensive, hierarchical framework for mapping stakeholders, their roles, and the benefits they derive from Open Research Information (ORI) in the context of the Barcelona Declaration. Unlike traditional research metrics that focus solely on outputs (papers, projects), this taxonomy makes visible the complex ecosystem of actors and the multidimensional value generated throughout the research lifecycle.

The taxonomy is structured around **eight core benefit dimensions**, which are then mapped to **stakeholder categories** through **functional roles**. This approach enables:
- Clear identification of value propositions for different actors
- Evidence-based advocacy for ORI adoption
- Structured case study collection and analysis
- Policy development aligned with stakeholder needs

## 2. Core Benefit Dimensions

These eight dimensions represent the fundamental value categories that ORI generates for the research ecosystem. Each benefit addresses a specific challenge in the current research information landscape.

### **B1: Research Output Quality & Traceability**
**Definition:** Enhances the visibility, credibility, and traceability of research products throughout their lifecycle.

**Key Aspects:**
- Complete provenance tracking from data to publication
- Enhanced discoverability through structured metadata
- Improved citation and attribution mechanisms
- Version control and reproducibility support
- Cross-linking of related research objects

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Traditional metrics count publications but don't trace the complete research process, making replication difficult and hiding the interconnected nature of research outputs.

---

### **B2: Governance, Ethics & Compliance**
**Definition:** Enhances internal policies, ethical standards, and compliance with open and responsible research norms.

**Key Aspects:**
- Transparent research conduct monitoring
- Ethics approval tracking and compliance verification
- Data protection and privacy compliance
- Open science policy implementation support
- Institutional accountability mechanisms

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Compliance and ethical practices are invisible in publication counts, yet they are fundamental to research integrity.

---

### **B3: Capacity Building & Inclusive Training**
**Definition:** Builds inclusive skills, literacy, and institutional capabilities, especially supporting underrepresented actors.

**Key Aspects:**
- Skills development in open research practices
- Metadata literacy programs
- Support for researchers from diverse backgrounds
- Training in FAIR data principles
- Institutional capacity strengthening

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Learning processes and skill development are completely absent from traditional metrics, yet they are critical for sustainable research ecosystems.

---

### **B4: Interoperability & Metadata Quality**
**Definition:** Strengthens data interoperability, metadata completeness, and standard adoption.

**Key Aspects:**
- Harmonized metadata schemas across systems
- Persistent identifier (PID) adoption and implementation
- Standardized vocabularies and ontologies
- System integration and API development
- Cross-platform data exchange

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** The infrastructure enabling research connectivity is invisible in traditional metrics, despite being essential for modern collaborative research.

---

### **B5: Evaluation for Learning & Responsibility**
**Definition:** Informs institutional, national, and global evaluation processes that support learning, equity, and accountability.

**Key Aspects:**
- Formative assessment approaches
- Multi-dimensional research impact evaluation
- Equity-focused evaluation frameworks
- Organizational learning mechanisms
- Responsible research assessment practices

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Traditional metrics drive competitive ranking rather than learning; they measure outputs but not the quality of research processes or their societal alignment.

---

### **B6: Visibility, Equity & Societal Relevance**
**Definition:** Increases equitable access, civic engagement, and alignment with societal needs and Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

**Key Aspects:**
- Research accessibility for non-academic audiences
- Citizen science enablement
- Geographic and linguistic equity
- SDG alignment tracking
- Public engagement and science communication

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Societal impact and public engagement are systematically undervalued in traditional metrics, which privilege academic citations.

---

### **B7: Infrastructure & Technical Enablement**
**Definition:** Supports or enhances the platforms and services that enable open research.

**Key Aspects:**
- Sustainable infrastructure development
- Technical standards implementation
- Service interoperability
- Platform reliability and performance
- Innovation in research tools

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** The technical foundations enabling research are completely invisible in traditional output metrics.

---

### **B8: Integrity & Accountability**
**Definition:** Fosters transparency, accountability, and ethical conduct throughout the research lifecycle.

**Key Aspects:**
- Research misconduct detection and prevention
- Transparent funding and conflict of interest disclosure
- Audit trails and documentation
- Public accountability mechanisms
- Trust-building in research systems

**Traditional Metrics Gap:** Traditional metrics cannot reveal ethical issues, conflicts of interest, or questionable research practices—they only count outputs regardless of integrity.

## 3. Stakeholder Categories

Stakeholder categories organize actors by their primary institutional or organizational context.

### **3.1 Research-Performing Institutions**
Organizations that conduct research as a core function.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Universities
- Independent research centers
- Academic departments
- Private sector research divisions
- Hospital-based research units

**Primary Concerns:** Research quality, reputation, talent attraction, funding success, societal impact

---

### **3.2 Individual Researchers**
Persons directly engaged in research activities.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Early-career researchers
- Senior researchers
- Principal investigators
- Interdisciplinary teams
- Citizen scientists

**Primary Concerns:** Career development, research visibility, collaboration opportunities, skill development, recognition

---

### **3.3 Funders**
Organizations that provide financial support for research.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- National research agencies
- International cooperation bodies
- Philanthropic foundations
- Private sector funders
- Crowdfunding platforms

**Primary Concerns:** Impact accountability, strategic alignment, portfolio management, transparency, value for investment

---

### **3.4 Research Infrastructure Providers**
Organizations that supply technical platforms and services.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Repositories (data, publications, software)
- PID infrastructure providers (DOI, ORCID, ROR)
- Metadata and indexing services
- Information service providers
- Research information systems (CRIS) providers

**Primary Concerns:** Service sustainability, technical standards, user adoption, interoperability, data quality

---

### **3.5 Policy Makers and Evaluators**
Organizations that set policy or conduct assessment.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Government ministries
- Research assessment agencies
- Accreditation bodies
- Think tanks
- Policy advisory groups

**Primary Concerns:** Evidence-based policymaking, accountability, strategic planning, impact assessment, resource allocation

---

### **3.6 Civil Society and NGOs**
Non-governmental organizations representing public interests.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Patient associations
- Citizen science organizations
- Open knowledge networks
- Special interest groups (lawyers, patients, etc.)
- Community-based organizations

**Primary Concerns:** Access to research, societal relevance, participation, health/social outcomes, equity

---

### **3.7 Libraries and Research Managers**
Professionals supporting research administration and information management.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- University libraries
- Data stewards
- CRIS managers
- Research support officers
- Information specialists

**Primary Concerns:** Service delivery, metadata quality, policy compliance, user support, system integration

---

### **3.8 Publishers and Journals**
Organizations disseminating research outputs.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Commercial publishers
- Society-led journals
- Institutional journals
- Diamond open access publishers
- Preprint servers

**Primary Concerns:** Business sustainability, metadata quality, discoverability, ethical publishing, community trust

---

### **3.9 Technology and Service Providers**
Companies and organizations providing technical solutions.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- Platform developers
- API and integration service providers
- Data analytics companies
- Software vendors
- Cloud service providers

**Primary Concerns:** Market opportunities, technical standards, interoperability, innovation, customer needs

---

### **3.10 Intergovernmental Organizations**
Global bodies coordinating international research efforts.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- UNESCO
- OECD
- EOSC (European Open Science Cloud)
- World Health Organization
- World Bank research divisions

**Primary Concerns:** Global coordination, standard harmonization, capacity building, equity, policy alignment

---

### **3.11 Networks and Alliances**
Collaborative organizations promoting open research practices.

**Specific Stakeholders:**
- GO FAIR
- Research Data Alliance (RDA)
- Confederation of Open Access Repositories (COAR)
- LA Referencia
- Regional scholarly communication networks

**Primary Concerns:** Community building, practice sharing, standard adoption, advocacy, collective action

## 4. Functional Roles

Functional roles describe **how** stakeholders interact with ORI, independent of their category. A single stakeholder may perform multiple roles.

### **4.1 Producer**
**Definition:** Creates or contributes directly to research outputs, data, or metadata.

**Activities:**
- Conducting research and generating data
- Creating publications and preprints
- Developing software and tools
- Documenting research processes
- Contributing metadata

**Primary Benefits:** B1, B2, B3

---

### **4.2 Evaluator**
**Definition:** Assesses the quality, impact, or compliance of research and institutions.

**Activities:**
- Conducting research assessments
- Peer review and quality evaluation
- Impact analysis
- Compliance auditing
- Performance benchmarking

**Primary Benefits:** B1, B3, B5

---

### **4.3 Enabler**
**Definition:** Provides platforms, infrastructure, or services that make open research possible.

**Activities:**
- Operating repositories and databases
- Providing persistent identifiers
- Developing technical standards
- Building integration tools
- Maintaining research platforms

**Primary Benefits:** B1, B4, B7

---

### **4.4 Policy Maker**
**Definition:** Defines, mandates, or influences strategies and frameworks for governance.

**Activities:**
- Setting open research policies
- Creating regulatory frameworks
- Establishing funding requirements
- Defining assessment criteria
- Advocating for systemic change

**Primary Benefits:** B2, B5, B8

---

### **4.5 Support & Stewardship**
**Definition:** Maintains metadata, provides training, and ensures quality and alignment.

**Activities:**
- Metadata curation and quality control
- Training and capacity building
- Policy implementation support
- Research support services
- Documentation and guidance

**Primary Benefits:** B1, B2

---

### **4.6 Advocate / Civil Actor**
**Definition:** Promotes accessibility, inclusion, and societal relevance of research.

**Activities:**
- Public engagement and advocacy
- Promoting research accessibility
- Supporting underrepresented communities
- Connecting research to societal needs
- Citizen science participation

**Primary Benefits:** B1, B6, B8

---

### **4.7 Coordinator / Facilitator**
**Definition:** Connects actors, aligns practices, and enables interoperability at scale.

**Activities:**
- Building collaborative networks
- Harmonizing standards and practices
- Facilitating knowledge exchange
- Coordinating multi-stakeholder initiatives
- Promoting collective action

**Primary Benefits:** B4, B6, B8

---

### **4.8 Data Analyst**
**Definition:** Analyzes research information to generate insights and inform decisions.

**Activities:**
- Research trend analysis
- Impact assessment and metrics
- Policy evidence generation
- Performance reporting
- Strategic intelligence

**Primary Benefits:** B5, B7

## 6. Usage Guidelines

### **6.1 For Case Study Collection**
When documenting ORI benefits:
1. Identify the stakeholder(s) involved
2. Specify their category and role(s)
3. Map to relevant benefit dimension(s)
4. Describe concrete outcomes and evidence

### **6.2 For Advocacy and Communication**
When communicating with stakeholders:
1. Identify their category and primary concerns
2. Emphasize benefits aligned with their role(s)
3. Use concrete examples from similar stakeholders
4. Connect to their strategic objectives

### **6.3 For Policy Development**
When designing ORI policies:
1. Map affected stakeholder categories
2. Consider their diverse roles and needs
3. Ensure policies deliver relevant benefits
4. Design multi-stakeholder engagement processes

### **6.4 For System Design**
When building ORI infrastructure:
1. Identify stakeholder categories to be served
2. Design features supporting their roles
3. Ensure benefit delivery is measurable
4. Enable interoperability across stakeholder contexts

## 8. Relationship to Barcelona Declaration Principles

This taxonomy operationalizes the four core commitments of the Barcelona Declaration:

| **Barcelona Declaration Commitment** | **Taxonomy Support** |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------|
| **1. Make openness the default** | Benefits B1, B6, B8 demonstrate value of openness; stakeholder mapping identifies adoption pathways |
| **2. Work with supporting services** | Roles: Enabler, Support & Stewardship; Benefits B4, B7 highlight infrastructure needs |
| **3. Support sustainability** | Benefit B7 addresses infrastructure sustainability; multi-stakeholder approach ensures ecosystem resilience |
| **4. Support collective action** | Role: Coordinator/Facilitator; Benefits B4, B6 emphasize collaborative value creation |

### 8.1 Alignment with Global Open Science Initiatives

This taxonomy is designed to complement and support major global initiatives for open research and research assessment reform:

#### **Plan S / cOAlition S**
[Plan S](https://www.coalition-s.org/), launched in 2018 by cOAlition S (a consortium of research funders), mandates immediate open access to publications from publicly funded research. The taxonomy supports Plan S implementation by:
- Identifying stakeholder roles in open publishing ecosystems (B1, B7)
- Mapping benefits for funders and research institutions (B2, B5, B8)
- Supporting compliance monitoring and evaluation (B2, B5)

#### **Coalition for Advancing Research Assessment (CoARA)**
[CoARA](https://www.coara.org/), launched in December 2022 with over 700 signatories, promotes reform of research assessment based on 10 core commitments. This taxonomy operationalizes CoARA principles by:
- Providing multi-dimensional benefits beyond publication metrics (B1-B8)
- Supporting qualitative peer review approaches (B5)
- Recognizing diverse contributions and outputs (B1, B3, B6)
- Enabling responsible use of quantitative indicators in context

#### **Open Science Monitoring Initiative (OSMI)**
[OSMI](https://open-science-monitoring.org/), coordinated by Université de Lorraine, SPARC Europe, and UNESCO following the December 2023 UNESCO conference in Paris, establishes principles for monitoring open science practices. The taxonomy aligns with OSMI's three pillars:
- **Relevance and significance:** Benefits linked to research quality and societal impact (B1, B6)
- **Transparency and reproducibility:** Open metadata and traceability (B1, B4, B8)
- **Self-assessment and responsible use:** Evaluation for learning (B5) and capacity building (B3)

#### **PathOS - Open Science Impact Pathways**
[PathOS](https://pathos-project.eu/) (EU Horizon Europe Grant 101058728, 2022-2025) identified and quantified open science impact pathways across science, society, and economy. The project has concluded, with results available through the [Open Science Impact Indicator Handbook](https://handbook.pathos-project.eu/). The taxonomy provides a complementary stakeholder-centered framework to PathOS's impact measurement approach:
- Stakeholder categories map to PathOS impact domains
- Benefit dimensions align with PathOS impact indicators
- Both frameworks support evidence-based ORI advocacy

## Appendices

### **Appendix A: Glossary of Terms**

**Open Research Information (ORI):** Structured, machine-readable metadata about all stages and outputs of the research lifecycle, made openly available for reuse.

**Stakeholder:** Any individual, organization, or entity that participates in, influences, or benefits from the open research information ecosystem.

**Functional Role:** The manner in which a stakeholder interacts with ORI, independent of their institutional or organizational category.

**Benefit Dimension:** A category of value or positive outcome generated by ORI adoption and implementation.

**Taxonomy:** A hierarchical classification system that organizes entities (in this case, stakeholders, roles, and benefits) into structured categories.

### **Appendix B: Mapping Methodology**

The relationships in this taxonomy were developed through:
1. Literature review of existing stakeholder and benefit frameworks
2. Analysis of Barcelona Declaration working group discussions
3. Consultation with Task Force 2 members
4. Validation through initial case study collection
5. Alignment with Barcelona Declaration principles and commitments
