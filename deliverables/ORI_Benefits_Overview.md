# The Value of Open Research Information: Eight Transformative Benefits

**Barcelona Declaration Working Group 7 - Task Force 2**
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** February 2026

---

## The Transformation: From Counting Outputs to Valuing Processes

For decades, research evaluation has been dominated by a single moment: **publication**. We count papers, measure citations, and calculate impact factors. Yet this narrow focus renders invisible the rich, multidimensional reality of modern research—the collaborative processes, ethical practices, skill development, infrastructure, and societal engagement that make research meaningful and trustworthy.

**The paradox is stark:** While research has become more interconnected, interdisciplinary, and socially engaged, our ability to see and value these dimensions has actually diminished. Traditional metrics systematically obscure:

- **The research process:** Everything that happens before publication—collaboration, data collection, ethical review, methodology development
- **Diverse contributions:** Data stewardship, software development, training, infrastructure maintenance
- **Societal impact:** Public engagement, policy influence, equitable access, alignment with sustainable development goals
- **Research integrity:** Ethical conduct, transparency, accountability, responsible practices

**Open Research Information (ORI)** offers a fundamental shift: structured, machine-readable metadata about research actors, activities, inputs, outputs, and impacts—made openly available through open infrastructures using standard protocols and identifiers. The Barcelona Declaration commits signatories to four actions: (1) **making openness the default**, (2) **working with open systems**, (3) **supporting infrastructure sustainability**, and (4) **enabling collective action**.

This document presents **eight benefit dimensions** that these commitments unlock, organized into three transformative axes: **Quality & Trust**, **Collaboration & Innovation**, and **Impact & Relevance**.

**A revealing symptom:** Research organizations are drowning in meetings. Evaluators need meetings to understand what researchers actually did. Funders need meetings to assess project progress. Administrators need meetings to understand resource needs. Why? Because our information systems don't make research processes visible. When metadata is absent, understanding requires constant verbal explanation. ORI transforms this: what once required synchronous meetings becomes asynchronous, documented, and reusable.

## Axis I: Quality & Trust

### B1: Research Output Quality & Traceability

**The Challenge:** Traditional metrics count publications but don't trace the complete research process, making replication difficult and hiding the interconnected nature of research outputs.

**ORI Solution:** Complete provenance tracking from data to publication, enhanced discoverability through structured metadata, improved citation and attribution mechanisms, version control for reproducibility, and cross-linking of related research objects.

**Value Created:**
- Researchers verify and build upon each other's work with confidence
- Institutions demonstrate research quality beyond citation counts
- The intellectual journey from question to answer becomes visible and valued

### B2: Governance, Ethics & Compliance

**The Challenge:** Ethical practices and compliance are invisible in publication counts, yet they are fundamental to research integrity.

**ORI Solution:** Transparent research conduct monitoring, ethics approval tracking, data protection compliance verification, open science policy implementation support, and institutional accountability mechanisms—following the principle of "as open as possible, as closed as necessary" to protect sensitive information while maximizing transparency.

**Value Created:**
- Organizations can demonstrate responsible research practices systematically
- Funders can verify alignment with ethical and regulatory requirements
- Public trust in research increases through visible accountability

### B8: Integrity & Accountability

**The Challenge:** Traditional metrics cannot reveal ethical issues, conflicts of interest, or questionable research practices—they only count outputs regardless of integrity.

**ORI Solution:** Research misconduct detection and prevention, transparent funding and conflict of interest disclosure, comprehensive audit trails, public accountability mechanisms, and trust-building throughout research systems.

**Value Created:**
- Early warning systems for research integrity issues become possible
- Transparent documentation deters misconduct and builds community trust
- Research systems become self-correcting through visible practices

## Axis II: Collaboration & Innovation

### B3: Capacity Building & Inclusive Training

**The Challenge:** Learning processes and skill development are completely absent from traditional metrics, yet they are critical for sustainable research ecosystems.

**ORI Solution:** Skills development in open research practices, metadata literacy programs, support for researchers from diverse backgrounds, training in FAIR data principles, and institutional capacity strengthening.

**Value Created:**
- Career development pathways beyond publication become visible and valued
- Underrepresented researchers gain recognition for their growing expertise
- Research communities build sustainable capabilities for open practices

### B4: Interoperability & Metadata Quality

**The Challenge:** The infrastructure enabling research connectivity is invisible in traditional metrics, despite being essential for modern collaborative research.

**ORI Solution:** Harmonized metadata schemas across systems, persistent identifier (PID) adoption, standardized vocabularies and ontologies, system integration and API development, and cross-platform data exchange.

**Value Created:**
- Research becomes findable, accessible, and reusable across institutional boundaries
- Collaboration efficiency increases through reduced friction in data exchange
- Global research infrastructure becomes coherent rather than fragmented

### B7: Infrastructure & Technical Enablement

**The Challenge:** The technical foundations enabling research are completely invisible in traditional output metrics.

**ORI Solution:** Sustainable infrastructure development, technical standards implementation, service interoperability, platform reliability and performance, and innovation in research tools.

**Value Created:**
- The "plumbing" of research becomes recognized as essential intellectual work
- Long-term sustainability of research platforms becomes strategic priority
- Technical innovation in research methods gains visibility and support

---

## Axis III: Impact & Relevance

### B5: Evaluation for Learning & Responsibility

**The Challenge:** Traditional metrics drive competitive ranking rather than learning; they measure outputs but not the quality of research processes or their societal alignment.

**ORI Solution:** Formative assessment approaches, multi-dimensional research impact evaluation, equity-focused evaluation frameworks, organizational learning mechanisms, and responsible research assessment practices.

**Value Created:**
- Evaluation shifts from judgment to improvement, from counting to understanding
- Research assessment supports career development rather than merely gatekeeping
- Organizations learn what works and why, enabling continuous improvement

### B6: Visibility, Equity & Societal Relevance

**The Challenge:** Societal impact and public engagement are systematically undervalued in traditional metrics, which privilege academic citations.

**ORI Solution:** Research accessibility for non-academic audiences, citizen science enablement, geographic and linguistic equity, SDG alignment tracking, and public engagement and science communication.

**Value Created:**
- Research reaches those who can benefit from it, not just other academics
- Communities worldwide participate in and contribute to research
- Research demonstrates alignment with urgent societal challenges like SDGs

---

## The Ecosystem: Enablers and Actors

These eight benefits emerge through coordinated action of diverse **stakeholders** (research institutions, researchers, funders, infrastructure providers, policymakers, civil society, libraries, publishers, technology providers, intergovernmental bodies, and networks) performing specific **roles** (producers, evaluators, enablers, policy makers, support staff, advocates, coordinators, and analysts).

**No single stakeholder can realize all benefits alone**—ORI value emerges from ecosystem-wide participation, where each actor contributes according to their capabilities and benefits according to their needs.

---

## Conclusion: From Invisibility to Visibility

Traditional metrics have created a crisis of invisibility, hiding what matters most: quality, integrity, collaboration, and societal relevance. **ORI makes the complete research lifecycle visible through comprehensive, transparent, machine-readable metadata.**

The eight benefit dimensions—**Quality & Trust**, **Collaboration & Innovation**, and **Impact & Relevance**—demonstrate that ORI is not merely infrastructure. It is a fundamental reimagining of how we recognize, value, and support research in the digital age.

The Barcelona Declaration's four commitments create the pathway for this transformation. By making processes visible, we can reward the full spectrum of contributions that make research trustworthy, impactful, and aligned with societal needs.

**The future of research evaluation lies not in counting more precisely, but in seeing more completely.**
